# Where Is My Visa Application?

The quickest answer usually sits with the government that is deciding your application. Choose that country below and its page takes you to the official way to check, with the reference details to have ready. Some governments run an online status checker; others only contact you when they decide.

## How do you check a UK visa application?

UKVI has no public online application tracker. It sends you an email or letter when it has made a decision, or if your application will take longer than usual. Until then, check your inbox and your junk folder before you contact anyone.

The UKVI contact centre can only give you an update on your application if both of these apply:

1. UKVI has taken longer than usual to process your application.
2. UKVI has not already contacted you about it.

Staff cannot tell you when you will get a decision. GOV.UK publishes current processing times for each visa type, with separate times for the priority and super priority services.

From outside the UK, [contacting UKVI](https://www.gov.uk/contact-ukvi-inside-outside-uk/y/outside-the-uk/checking-when-you-will-get-your-visa-decision) costs money. An email query costs £2.74, and follow-up emails about the same query are free. UKVI says it replies within 5 days. Calls cost 69 pence per minute on top of your network charges. A paid query is only worth it once your application has passed the usual processing time and UKVI has not written to you.

After a grant, you view your eVisa through a UKVI account. To [set up the account](https://www.gov.uk/evisa/set-up-ukvi-account), you can use a valid passport and your visa application number, which is your GWF or UAN number. You cannot see the eVisa until it is linked to the account. The [United Kingdom](https://masterfastvisas.com/visa-status-check/united-kingdom) page covers the details.

## How do you check a US visa application?

Use the State Department's [CEAC Visa Status Check](https://ceac.state.gov/CEACStatTracker/Status.aspx). If the consular officer has put your case into administrative processing under section 221(g), CEAC shows it as "Refused". That status can change once the officer's outstanding questions are resolved.

A "Refused" status after your interview can still change. The State Department [explains the change](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visas-news-archive/visas-ceac-case-status-change.html): applicants in administrative processing now see "Refused", and the status may change if you can show the consular officer you are eligible. Follow the instructions the officer gave you at the end of the interview. The [United States](https://masterfastvisas.com/visa-status-check/usa) page lists what to enter in CEAC.

## What if your application is taking too long?

First compare the time so far with the government's published processing time for your visa type. If you have passed it and heard nothing, use the official contact route on your country's page.

If you paid UKVI for the priority or super priority service, use the time published for that service, not the standard one. A query before that point rarely gets an answer, and on a UK application the contact centre cannot give you a decision date.

If your trip can't wait, MasterFastVisas can process a new priority e-visa in under 48 hours. The e-visa is a new application, not a way to speed up one you have already made. [Contact MasterFastVisas](https://masterfastvisas.com/contact-us) to ask whether your destination issues e-visas.
