# Rules card (what the writer needs; RULES.md is the full reference, and `next` checks everything)

**The page**
1. Follow `write_brief.md` section 1 (page job): on a service, product, category, location or home page the client's offer and its next step come early and at the close; official and other sites are cited evidence, never the reader's destination; never write against the client. A guide answers fully; where the reader's task meets the client's offer, one short passage and its link.
2. Write every heading of the plan, in its order, as natural English a person would ask or write: a plan heading taken from a search phrase that isn't grammatical ("How to compound interest calculate in excel") becomes "How do you calculate compound interest in Excel?", keeping its key words and names in their proper case. Each answers its question in its first one or two sentences (about 40 words, direct, using the heading's own terms; write each H2 as the reader's question or a "How to…" task), then the detail. "also answer" lines are answered inside that section in those words. No FAQ block, no "Introduction", "Overview" or "Conclusion".
3. Facts: only the ones in the brief, in your own sentences (never a sentence lifted from a page; a common 8-word phrase now and then is fine, 12 or more words in a row never), figures exactly as their notes give them, with the condition they carry (who, when, which product). No figure from memory. Never a losing figure from "Settled disagreements".
3b. Beyond page one: use every `gain:` item under its heading (that is the page's information gain), crediting who says it in the sentence ("a member of a trade forum found…", "an independent consumer test in 2025 showed…") and linking the source on those words.
3c. Sources and quotes: link the source wherever its fact is used (the page cites at least 3 different outside sources when the brief has them); where the plan gives a quote slot, put that fact's exact words from "Quotable, word for word" in quotation marks, with the source named and linked in the same sentence. Never change a quoted word.
3d. Your view: where the plan says "your view", give your own opinion in the first person ("I'd pick…", "I wouldn't bother with…", "My choice for a north-facing fence is…"): what you'd choose or do and why, from the facts and the experiences in the brief, and when you'd choose differently. Firm, specific, varied in wording (no stock disclaimer). 2-3 such passages on the page, in the top and middle sections only, never in the opening or the last sections. Never claim you used, tried, tested, bought or saw it, "in my experience", "my clients" or years of experience.
4. Links: the ones listed under each heading, the anchor being the target page's title (or its head phrase), inside a sentence about it, never in the first sentence under a heading; link an authority on the words a reader would check; each page once; never a competitor.

**Sentences**
5. Address the reader as "you". Short declarative sentences, one idea each, no nested clauses (average about 12–18 words, with variation).
5b. Koray's rules as habits, not a template (RULES.md section 7): main subject and its key attributes first; be certain (state the fact or the instruction with its condition, rather than "should", "might", "we'd recommend"); numbers instead of "many", "several"; examples right after a plural noun ("such as A, B and C"); list items start the same way; "if" clauses mostly second ("Do X if Y"); bold the answer items, never the search term; specific entities and the field's own verbs. A human expert breaks these now and then, and so may you: a few "If…" sentences (about one per 500 words), a hedge or a vague count once or twice, a statement heading among the questions, one list that mixes forms. The first sentence under every heading is the exception: it answers directly, with no "If…", hedge or vague count.
5e. Active voice: name who does it ("Councils set the fee", not "The fee is set by councils"). About 2 passive sentences a page at most.
5c. Human ideas (University of Maryland / Google DeepMind research): every sentence carries something only this page could say: a figure, a name, a product, a place, the case it applies to. Name the source ("the regulator found", linked), never "experts say" or "studies show". Say when advice doesn't apply, who it doesn't suit, what it costs. No perfect, hassle-free or all-needs solutions; no "will always/never" forecasts; no "Imagine…" or invented customer examples; no closing lesson ("The key takeaway…", "Ultimately…"); no reason that repeats the claim.
6. Name the thing: never open with a vague "This", "It" or "These" + verb. Name the actor of an opening "-ing" or "-ed" phrase.
7. No trailing commentary (", making it…", ", allowing you to…", ", ensuring…"), no teaser sentences ("Here's what to know."), no meta-commentary ("below", "this guide"), no colon reveals ("The catch: …"), no "not just/not only X but Y", no rule-of-three padding.
8. Banned words (and their forms): streamline, empower, delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, realm, vibrant, game-changer, cutting-edge, unparalleled, meticulous, leverage, foster, embark, elevate, unlock, seamless, effortless, robust, navigate, journey, deep dive, world-class, renowned, premier, tailored to, cater to, additionally, moreover, furthermore, "it's worth noting", "it's important to note", "plays a vital/crucial role", "in conclusion", "in today's…". Say the specific thing instead.
9. Headings in sentence case (capital on the first word and names only), under about 70 characters. Paragraphs of 2–4 sentences, one context each. At most one em dash per 150 words; no exclamation marks.
10. Scope (section 1 of the brief): on a **global** page write for readers anywhere (no "UK" framing; a rule that applies in one country names it; examples may use any currency); on a country page, that country's rules and sources. When the plan lists several needs the query mixes, open with a compact list: each term, what it is for, and the link to our calculator for it.
10b. The locale's spelling, units and currency (its own spelling, units and currency: colour/metric/£ for the UK, color/imperial/$ for the US, and so on). One name and one spelling per thing.
11. Money, health, legal and safety pages: the caution a professional must give, once, in the section where the decision is made, in concrete terms. No boilerplate disclaimers.

**Never**: mention the research ("top results", "other guides"), invent experience, statistics, credentials or customer stories, use HTML, or copy a competitor's wording (a sentence of someone else's words, even rearranged slightly, is rewritten in yours). Never name or link another online calculator website. A bank's or a brand's own calculator or product may be described (how it works, what changed), never linked.
